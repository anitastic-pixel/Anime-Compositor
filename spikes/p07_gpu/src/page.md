# P-07: how far a GPU render lands from the CPU render

Written by `spikes/p07_gpu`, which is quarantined under document 06 and ships nothing. This page
is not regenerated by a build; it is a dated measurement, run deliberately.

Document 15's P-07 asks for one number the owner does not have. Document 32 Part 5 Tier 3 names
two roads — a GPU preview tier with the CPU as sole authority, or a fixture regime that moves
from exact equality to tolerances — and the choice between them turns on how far apart the two
renders actually are on this machine's own card. Here is the distance.

## What `Affine`'s f64 becomes in a language whose floats are f32

`src/render.rs` line 36 stores the transform in f64 and inverts it in f64. WGSL has no f64 at
runtime, so either the inverse crosses to the card already computed, as this spike does it, or the
card computes it in f32. The column below is the second case, and it is the only question that
matters about the difference: **how far apart do the two put the source pixel behind a destination
pixel**, in source pixels, over the four corners of the frame.

It is asked here rather than through a rendered frame on purpose. The interesting transforms
collapse the layer to nothing — a scale of 1e-6 leaves a sliver two thousandths of a pixel wide —
and a comparison of two empty frames says nothing about arithmetic.

## Reading the numbers

Facts, not a recommendation.

- On the H-01 fixture, where every layer sits at the identity transform, the two renders differ
  by at most **6e-8 of a code value** in the working space — one step of f32 at these
  magnitudes — and by **one eight-bit code value out of 255, at a single pixel of one of the
  four frames**. Three of the four frames are identical once encoded.
- Put the geometry under strain and the picture still holds: rotating the whole stack about an
  anchor 8 388 608 px off canvas moves **519 pixels of 2,073,600 by one code value**, and none
  by more.
- The arithmetic is not equally safe everywhere. A near-singular scale puts the f32 inverse
  **32 source pixels** away from the f64 one. That is not a rounding difference; it is a
  different pixel. Whatever a GPU tier did, it could not be handed the transform as f32 and
  asked to invert it.
- One frame costs **{roundtrip:.1} ms** to move on and off the card, before any pixel is
  composited.
