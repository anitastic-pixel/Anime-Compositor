D-234 / One 3D colour table for a stack of colour effects, preview only / **PROPOSED on 2026-09-29 by the agent**, as the deferred half of G13 (the owner's "defer if not to our standard/to your discretion", 2026-09-29). **The proposal.** A drawing's run of per-pixel colour effects (Curves, Levels, Hue/Saturation, Brightness/Contrast, Tint and the like) is one function of the pixel's encoded straight colour, because the linear round trip between two of them is the identity on a held value. The viewer would sample that function on an N by N by N grid of encoded colours and read each pixel back by `core.color_lookup`'s trilinear rule (D-182's `.cube` rule), rebuilding the table when a setting changes; export would keep the exact chain. **What was measured.** `tools/d234_lut_collapse_experiment.py`, its vectorised effects checked against the curves, levels, hue_saturation, brightness_contrast and cube_lut reference tools first (Tint from `src/effects.rs`), on the reference frame `verification/B-05a_reference_frame.png` and on all 16,777,216 8-bit colours, as the viewer shows them in 8-bit sRGB. A mild five-effect grade: grid 17 worst 9 levels on the frame (11 over all colours), 21.8% of pixels off by more than 1, PSNR 48.8 dB; grid 33 worst 5 (6), 2.8%, 56.1 dB; grid 65 worst 3 (3), 0.18%, 60.4 dB. A hard grade: 17 worst 27 (38), 27.3%, 42.0 dB; 33 worst 14 (18), 10.6%, 49.0 dB; 65 worst 8 (12), 1.6%, 58.6 dB. Each effect alone over all colours: Curves, Levels and Tint within 1 level at 33 and 65, Brightness/Contrast at 65 only, Hue/Saturation up to 5 (mild) and 21 (hard) levels even at 65; the hard grade without Hue/Saturation is still 2 levels off at 65. **No grid meets the accepted 1 level.** **Speed, an estimate by operation counts, not a timing:** about 700 operations a pixel for the five-effect chain against about 170 for the table, some 4 times; but G13's fusion of the same maths into one pass, which changes no pixels, already removes most of the repeated sRGB round trips, leaving the table about 2 times ahead of it. The table costs 35,937 chain evaluations to build at 33 (about 2% of a 1920 by 1080 frame) and 274,625 at 65 (about 13%). **Recommendation: decline for now**, build G13 and measure; reopen only if a colour stack is still slow after it. **For the owner:** (1) decline for now (recommended); (2) a table used only where a check over every colour finds it within 1 level, preview only, off by default, at about 8 frames' work per setting change; (3) the 65 table as it is, preview only, off by default, accepting up to about 12 levels off in the viewer on hard grades. **The evidence.** `verification/D-234 proposal/` (`proposal.md`, the side-by-side pictures exact / table / difference x32 for both grades at 17, 33 and 65, `experiment_output.txt`). **If declined,** nothing is built and no pixel changes.

D-235 / Large Gaussian Blur, Glow and Bloom worked at a reduced size and enlarged, preview only / **PROPOSED on 2026-09-29 by the agent**, as the deferred half of G14 (the owner's "defer if not to our standard/to your discretion", 2026-09-29). **The proposal.** In the viewer, a Gaussian blur of sigma s (Gaussian Blur's own blur, Glow's at radius / 3, each of Bloom's four) may be worked by averaging f by f blocks (f = 2, 4 or 8), blurring the small picture at sqrt(s^2 - (f^2 - 1) / 12 - f^2 / 6) / f, which takes out the blur the shrinking and enlarging add, and enlarging it back bilinearly from pixel centres, outside the layer transparent as document 21 has it. **The safety rule:** f is halved, down to 1 (exact), until the small sigma is at least 6. Export keeps document 21's exact blur. **What was measured.** `tools/d235_downsample_blur_experiment.py`, its exact blur checked against `tools/adjust_reference.py`'s first, on `verification/B-05a_reference_frame.png` at 1920 by 1080, Glow and Bloom at their default thresholds and intensity 1, Bloom's streaks off, as the viewer shows the frame over black in 8-bit sRGB; sizes 20, 50, 100, 200 (Gaussian Blur's sigma, Glow's and Bloom's radius), factors 2, 4, 8. The plain shortcut, always at the factor asked, breaks 1 level at small sizes: Gaussian Blur 20 at factor 8, 3 levels; Glow 20 at 2, 4, 8: 3, 7, 18 levels (12.7% of pixels, 50.4 dB at 8); Glow 50 at 4 and 8: 2 and 4; Glow 100 at 8: 2; Bloom 20 at 2, 4, 8: 8, 30, 60 levels (28.2%, 35.0 dB at 8); Bloom 50: 3, 9, 22; Bloom 100 at 4 and 8: 2 and 10; Bloom 200 at 8: 3. Every other case is within 1 level with 0% of pixels off by more, PSNR 59.4 to 76.8 dB. So "above radius 50, factor 2" alone is not safe (Bloom 50 at factor 2 is 3 levels). **With the safety rule** every case measured is within 1 level with no pixel off by more: Gaussian Blur 50, 100, 200 at factor 8, 61.6 to 68.7 dB; Glow 50 at 2, 100 at 4, 200 at 8; Bloom 200 at 8/4/2/1 across its four blurs, 69.9 dB; Glow and Bloom at 20 stay exact. Guards of 2 and 4 each left cases 2 to 3 levels off. **Speed, an estimate by counts of multiply-adds per pixel, not a timing:** with the rule and factor up to 8, Gaussian Blur about 7x at 20, 97x at 50, 162x at 100, 247x at 200; Glow 1x, 6.6x, 35x, 122x; Bloom 1x, 1.8x, 3.8x, 10x. Real gains will be smaller where memory rather than arithmetic sets the pace, as on the card. **Recommendation: accept with the safety rule, viewer only, exports exact,** built with the blur, glow and bloom fixtures and this frame as its checks and left off until they pass on the card; one frame was tested, and a picture with harder bright edges may find a case it did not. **For the owner:** (1) accept with the safety rule, on in the viewer (recommended); (2) the same behind a viewer switch off by default; (3) only while playing or scrubbing, the exact frame replacing it on stop (with G9); (4) decline. **The evidence.** `verification/D-235 proposal/` (`proposal.md`, side-by-side pictures exact / shortcut / difference x16 for each effect at 20 factor 8, 50 factor 2, 100 factor 4 and 200 factor 8 plain, and 200 factor 8 with the rule, `experiment_output.txt`). **If declined,** nothing is built and big blurs stay exact and as slow as today.
